# CME trade-type and standalone-product handoff

Research date: 2026-09-05 · Work item 5 of
[the coverage plan](2026-09-05-cme-globex-family-coverage.md).

This is the deliverable for the 37 electronic shapes SharurPlatform's Databento GLBX audit left
without an authored selector. It is a **handoff**, not an implementation: it says, per root, which
`MarketHoursKey` should own it, what still needs sourcing, and — where the answer is unknown — the
specific document a future session must retrieve.

Six shape groups were researched independently and then adversarially challenged. **Four of six
challenges overturned or qualified their verdict**, including one that falsified the survey's only
supposed counterexample. Where that happened the corrected answer is given and the overturning is
recorded, because the reasoning is more reusable than the conclusion.

## The decision rule this produced

Every trade-type instrument surveyed closes at an instant that is **also** a settlement, marker or
fixing instant for its asset class — CME's *Daily Settlement Time Details* matches the observed
closes six-for-six in the metals/Treasury group and four-for-four in energy/agriculture. **So the
envelope alone can never answer whether a shape is its own family.**

> A trade-type shape earns its own key when **CME states the instant in session language** —
> "trading halts … daily", "trading ceases daily at", "TAS TRADING HOURS", "TRADING HOURS … BTIC:",
> a `closed@` event in the trading-hours service, or a stated daily break. It stays **UNMAPPED**
> when the only CME statement is a **calculation window** ("the marker is calculated from trade
> data from 12:34 – 12:35 p.m. London Time") or an **order-entry cutoff**, and the boundary is
> otherwise feed-only.

The corollary caught two near-misses: **"Termination of Trading" is expiry, not a daily close.** CME
prints it as a row *adjacent to* the hours row. Reading it as a session boundary is what nearly
produced six phantom event-contract families — see `SER-9624`, where five contracts with five
different termination times share one Trading Hours cell.

Out of roughly 180 trade-type roots surveyed, **exactly one** genuinely reuses an underlying's key.

## 3. The finding that matters most: variant vs. family

### 3.1 The one general rule that came out of all six groups

Every trade-type instrument surveyed here closes at an instant that is **also** a settlement, marker or fixing instant for its asset class. CME's *"Daily Settlement Time Details"* matches the observed closes six-for-six in the metals/Treasury group and four-for-four in the energy/ag group: Livestock `12:59:30-13:00:00 CT`, Grains/Oilseeds `13:14:00-13:15:00 CT`, Energy `14:28:00-14:30:00 ET`, Bitcoin `14:59:00-15:00:00 CT`, Gold `13:29:00-13:30:00 ET`, Silver `13:24:00-13:25:00 ET`, Platinum `13:03:00-13:05:00 ET`, Copper `12:59:00-13:00:00 ET`, Palladium `12:58:00-13:00:00 ET`, Treasuries `13:59:30-14:00:00 CT`.

**So the envelope alone can never answer the question.** The decision rule that actually worked, and that the next session should apply:

> A trade-type shape earns its own key when **CME states the instant in session language** — "trading halts … daily", "trading ceases daily at", "TAS TRADING HOURS", "TRADING HOURS … BTIC:", a `closed@` event in the trading-hours service, or a stated daily break. It stays UNMAPPED when the only CME statement is a **calculation window** ("the marker is calculated from trade data from 12:34 – 12:35 p.m. London Time") or an **order-entry cutoff**, and the boundary is otherwise feed-only.

The corollary that caught two near-misses: **"Termination of Trading" is expiry, not a daily close.** CME prints it as a distinct row *adjacent to* the hours row on the Japanese equity fact card, the TMAC page and the crypto BTIC FAQ. Reading it as a session boundary is the failure mode that nearly produced six phantom event-contract families.

### 3.2 Trade-type variants that genuinely reuse an underlying's key

**Exactly one, out of ~180 trade-type roots surveyed.**

- **`6EP`** (Euro FX BTIC+) → `globex_fx`. CME states it in words: *"BTIC+ contracts can be traded through the standard CME Globex trading hours of Sunday-Friday 5:00 p.m.-4:00 p.m. CT."* Its plain sibling `6EB` is not, and the same FAQ says why. Requires narrowing the `excludes … BTIC` clause at `futures_profile.rs:168-169` to exclude BTIC while admitting BTIC+.

**Two candidates that looked like reuse and are not:**

- **`AWT`/`BAT`/…/`CCT`** (commodity-index BTIC). The BTIC verdict cited `AW`/`AWT` sharing one exact envelope signature as the proof that a BTIC can ride the underlying's clock. The cash-products challenge falsified it: CME Submission 23-074 tabulates a *BTIC-specific* window for AW (`5:00 pm – 1:30pm CT`) that is not AW's Globex window (`8:15 am – 1:30 pm CT`), and the sibling pair `DRS`/`DRT` sits on 17:00–16:00 vs 17:00–13:30 CT in the same 2026-08-30 file. **The one supposed counterexample in the whole survey collapsed.**
- **`ES1`/`ES2`/`EQ1`** (equity BTIC+/TACO+). Their window *is* the standard 17:00–16:00 CT envelope — but `globex_equity_index` excludes BTIC/TACO products by name, and CME Globex Notice 2021-06-21 says *"all BTIC and TACO products will not be impacted"* by the 2021-06-28 halt removal, so their halt history diverges. Same envelope, different history → **own key**.

### 3.3 Genuinely distinct families

Everything else. In summary form:

| Shape class | Why distinct |
|---|---|
| **Equity/sector/AIR BTIC** (row 2) | Closes 16:00 ET vs the outright's 17:00 ET. CME prints the two lines **adjacent** on its own spec pages: *"CME Globex: Sunday 6:00 p.m. - Friday - 5:00 p.m. ET"* against *"BTIC: Sunday - Friday 6:00 p.m. - 4:00 p.m. ET"* |
| **Credit BTIC** (6 roots) | Same envelope as equity BTIC, different family (CBOT seg-84 Bloomberg credit, `"5:00 p.m. - 3:00 p.m. CT with a 60-minute break"`). One envelope is not one family |
| **Crypto BTIC** ×3 | 24/7 grid **plus** a 5-minute reference-rate stop at 15:00 / 10:00 / 03:00 CT, rolling the trade date at three different city closes. Two of the three anchors survive a DST probe as genuinely non-Chicago |
| **Nikkei vs TOPIX BTIC** | Identical published sentence, **different actual grid** — Nikkei carries a second `Noon – 5:00 p.m. ET` window, TOPIX does not. Two keys |
| **6EB** (FX BTIC) | Standard FX envelope **plus** a 50-minute London-anchored interruption the underlying does not have |
| **All TAS** | Grain TAS closes 13:15 CT vs `globex_grains` 13:20; livestock TAS 13:00 vs 13:05; energy TAS 13:30 vs 16:00; gold TAS 12:30 vs COMEX 16:00; crypto TAS rolls 15:00 CT vs crypto futures 16:00. Every one is an executable-window difference of 5 minutes to 4 hours |
| **All TAM** | Cross-zone by construction: Chicago open, London/Singapore/Shanghai close |
| **TACO, TMAC** | Own published grids (10:00 CT T-1 → 08:30 CT; 18:00 → 16:00 ET), both excluded by name from `globex_equity_index` |
| **The 10 standalone cash families** | Ordinary outright products that merely share the row-1 envelope or a day-session shape; each has its own rulebook chapter, launch date and (for CVB) its own dated divergence from a family it once matched |

**The "it's just derived" objection, and why it does not remove the work.** Every TAS envelope decomposes as *{underlying's open} + {end of that asset class's settlement determination range}*, and the livestock case proves the derivation (TAS inherited the underlying's open change in 2015/2016 and kept the settlement close). A reviewer can fairly say the crate is minting ~13 keys whose content is a formula. The answer is that the crate answers *"is this identity tradeable at instant t"*: at 14:00 CT a `CL` holder is trading and a `CLT` holder is not — group 80:CT is closed 13:30–16:45 CT and then runs a full Pre-Open/Ready cycle, which a fixing inside a session never produces. "Derived" explains why the divergence exists; it does not remove the need to model it.

**What the parent still owes a decision on: granularity.** CME's own TAS eligibility workbook (`/trading/files/tas-tam-eligibility.xlsx`) has exactly six tabs — *Cryptocurrency TAS / Energy TAS / Grain and Oilseed TAS / Livestock TAS / Metals TAS / Treasury TAS* — and its single "Energy TAS" sheet covers `CLT`, `MCT`, `NGT`, `HHT`, `7FT`, `TAS` and `TTS` together. That is one asset class where this handoff proposes three keys (`globex_energy_tas`, `globex_gasoil_tas`, plus an unmapped Dutch TTF). Decide that on family semantics and history, not on the envelope.
