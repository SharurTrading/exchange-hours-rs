## Evidence method

The review checked all 93 non-synthetic `Exchange` identities and all 11
operator-derived keys against 54 normalized source sets. Every real-market
primary source set contains an official current-hours or living-rulebook entry
point and an official notice/evidence channel; the synthetic-policy and FESE
secondary-corroboration sets are explicitly labeled exceptions. For every
modeled row the audit compared:

- IANA time zone and trading weekday mask;
- continuous, auction, order-entry, post-close, and trade-at-last phases;
- lunch, maintenance, transition, and weekend gaps;
- wraparound sessions and DST/reference-zone behavior;
- randomized auction boundaries and the documented nominal or conservative
  deterministic representation used by the crate;
- product, segment, participant, and security-eligibility scope; and
- every modeled historical cutover against a primary source with a day-level
  effective date.

Exact dated artifacts stay beside the Rust schedule table they support. The
source registry is intentionally a monitoring index rather than a duplicate
historical bibliography. A source that established a current grid but not its
start date was used to correct the current profile and record a Partial history
gap; it was never turned into an invented cutover.

## What the confidence statement means

Schedule-data confidence and query-engine confidence are separate:

- **Schedule data:** Primary and Partial current profiles have operator or
  regulator evidence and were manually compared field by field. Primary means
  no known modeled-history gap since January 2010. Partial means the current
  profile is supported but at least one older phase, era, or exact boundary is
  explicitly incomplete.
- **Query engine:** deterministic public-surface contracts sweep every exchange,
  session kind, and historical profile. The current and historical cross-query
  fences each execute more than 50,000 checks, in addition to exact cutover,
  DST, wrap, candle, maintenance, Chrono-boundary, serde, and per-venue tests.

That supports high confidence in all current normal-week answers within their
stated scope and high confidence that the engine applies those tables
consistently. It does not predict a future notice or extend the stated
exchange/product scopes to holidays or per-security microstructure.

`MarketHoursKey` retains fixed-current `session_profile` and
`hours_for_market_hours_key` snapshots, while
`hours_for_market_hours_key_as_of` reuses sourced product-family histories.
`calendar_for_market_hours_key` performs that reselection for every candidate
session-opening day during a scan.

The current CME cryptocurrency table stores the weekend in adjacent
one-midnight pieces. The identity-aware key calendar joins them into CME's
continuous multi-day bounds and assigns both blocks around Saturday maintenance
to the following open business date. That is normally Monday, with a Friday
16:01 Pre-Open→Monday 16:00 daily bar; a caller policy that closes Monday leaves the
weekend trading open, assigns it to Tuesday, and closes the bar Tuesday at
16:00. Friday 16:00 remains the final weekly close before the next trade-date
week enters Pre-Open at 16:01. Fixed `MarketHours` snapshots preserve exact open/closed
state but deliberately retain their table-piece bounds and expose neither that
weekly boundary nor a trade-date query because they carry no family identity.
Individual member-product listing dates after the family began are caller
instrument-catalog data.

## Exclusions

This crate models recurring normal-week schedules. This audit does not claim
coverage for holidays, early closes, one-off emergency closures, severe-weather
arrangements, halts, ad-hoc maintenance, or a product/segment outside the scope
written in its ledger row. A caller can overlay sourced closed days, early
final closes, and late first opens through `DayPolicy` or the validated
`StaticDayPolicy` table; the crate ships no operator exception dataset. These
boundary overlays do not claim to represent holiday windows that replace or
split phases; see [Date exceptions and holiday calendars](date-exceptions.md).
Randomized
auctions use the documented deterministic maximum or nominal edge; that
approximation is stated per venue.
